Eight quarters, one at a time

Forecast at the start of each of 8 quarters, the CRM defaults missed by 23% on average and learned probabilities by 61%, on public tender data.

What this addsEvery quarterLimits

What this adds

One quarter can flatter a forecast. In July to September 2025, the latest quarter scored, the CRM defaults called 440 wins and 426 came, a miss of +3%. Run from the start of each of the 7 quarters before it as well, the defaults were too high every time, by 3% to 50%, and 23% on average.

The defaults were the closest in 6 of 8 quarters. Learned probabilities were too low every time, by 50% to 68%. Time in stage was too low every time too, 28% on average, and last quarter's wins missed by 33%. On the 7 earlier quarters alone the averages were 25%, 61%, 28% and 32%.

Why the forecasts that learn from history all fall short: they learn from wins on record at the quarter's start, and many recent wins were not on record yet. Given every win known today, a backtest that ignores when wins were recorded, time in stage would have missed by only 13% and last quarter's wins by 16%. No team could have had those numbers at the time.

A correction scales each past quarter's wins by the share that would have been on record by then, using only older wins. Time in stage then missed by 16% on average and last quarter's wins by 18%. Quarter by quarter, time in stage with late wins counted was closer than the defaults in 4 of 8 and closer than last quarter's wins with the same count in 4 of 8, so these 8 quarters cannot tell the three apart.

Why the defaults look good. Over the 8 quarters, scored only on the wins from deals dated in the quarter, the only deals they count, the defaults were too high every time, by 140% on average. Over the same 8 quarters, learned probabilities missed those wins by 24% on average, too low every time. These misses are on different wins from the averages above, so they do not conflict. The overrating fills in for the wins from deals dated outside the quarter, 45% to 54% of the wins from open deals in each of the 8 quarters.

Ranges did not hold. An 80% range from each deal's odds covered the actual count in 1 of 8 quarters for the defaults and 0 for time in stage. The data cannot say why: the forecasts are biased, the defaults count fewer deals than they are scored on, the odds are estimates treated as known, and deals may move together. A range from the earlier quarters' misses covered 2 of 5, but those misses were scored with wins published later. Scored with only the wins published by each forecast date, the same range covered 5 of 5 for time in stage with late wins counted and 1 of 5 for the defaults. For the latest quarter it ran from 310 to 537 around a forecast of 392.

Limits

Each forecast used the awards as published by its quarter's start, but tender fields as last amended. Rebuilding those fields moved no average miss by more than 0.51 points.

With the deals whose notices were amended later left out, the defaults came in too high in only 2 of the 8 quarters, so their direction is not firm.

The defaults' accuracy depends on the stage mapping. One stage lower on each side (40% and 60%), they missed by 16% on average over the 8 quarters, and ran low in 6 of them.

Each range from earlier quarters' misses rests on only 3 to 7 of them, so it is wide. That the time in stage range covered 5 of 5 shows it was not too narrow, not that it is well sized.
